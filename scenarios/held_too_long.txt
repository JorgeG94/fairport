# What holding an arrival costs.
#
# `hold_arrival` takes an aircraft out of the landing order. It does not take
# it out of the sky: it keeps flying circuits, keeps burning fuel, and diverts
# at bingo like anybody else. That is the whole point. Holding is how a
# controller buys room, and the room is bought with somebody's fuel.
#
# Left alone, this day loses five:
#
#   MOR011 (M)  MOR014 (M)  MOR015 (H)  MOR017 (M)  MOR020 (H)
#
# MOR018 is not among them. It is a Medium in the middle of the morning bank,
# in no trouble at all, and holding it for the rest of the day loses it:
#
#   baseline              five diversions
#   hold_arrival 22       six, and the sixth is MOR018
#
# Nothing was gained. No aircraft landed that would not have landed anyway --
# the stack was already longer than the runway, so the slot MOR018 gave up went
# to somebody who was going to get one regardless. It is a pure loss, and it is
# the loss you take every time you hold an aircraft and then stop thinking
# about it.
#
# The interesting version is `at 08:00 hold_arrival 17`, which still loses
# five. MOR013 dies and MOR014 lives. MOR013 is the Super, carrying five
# hundred people instead of a hundred and eighty, and that trade is the same
# one `costly_favour.txt` makes from the other direction: there the player
# promotes an aircraft and costs the Super its slot, here they hold the Super
# and cost it directly. Two commands, opposite in shape, identical in outcome.
#
# There is no release here, which is the mistake being demonstrated. A held
# aircraft is not a parked one, and nothing reminds you.
#
#   fairport scenarios/full_day.txt        --seed 42
#   fairport scenarios/held_too_long.txt   --seed 42

seed 42
max_aircraft 128
load airport data/airports/simple.airport
load schedule data/schedules/day1.toml

# MOR018 is aircraft 22. Out of the landing order, and never let back in.
at 08:00 hold_arrival 22

run until 21:00

board
dump stats
